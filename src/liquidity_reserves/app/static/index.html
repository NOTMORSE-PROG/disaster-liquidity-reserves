        How the contraction scales

        A full joint table would hold 10N country states. The tensor chain touches a fixed number of entries per country and one payout register.

        How ReserveBound reaches a decision

        1. Model the payouts. Each country has calibrated earthquake and rain trigger rates and contractual caps. Each neighbouring pair shares its own earthquake and rain sources. Private rates are the marginal rates minus the shared rates, so every country's own event frequency stays fixed whatever the dependence.
        2. Contract the tensor chain. A country tensor links its payout to the shared shocks on its left and right. Sweeping along the chain adds each payout to a register and sums out a shared shock once both neighbours are done. The bond carries 10 states, whatever the number of countries.
        3. Bound the minimum reserve. Feasible scenarios (corners, cell centres, the base point) prove an amount is too small. For each cell of the range, a dominating model with the cell's highest shared and private rates proves an amount is enough everywhere in the cell. Only cells that can change the decision are split.
        4. Report the decision. When the bounds meet, the minimum is resolved. Otherwise the remaining bracket is returned with the scenario that defeats the lower amount.
